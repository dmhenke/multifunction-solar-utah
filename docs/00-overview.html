Multifunction Solar on Utah's Public Waters and Working Lands

A research-first strategy for solarizing public water bodies and working lands where solar has more than one function: power generation paired with water conservation, dust suppression, or ecosystem enhancement.

Design basis for water sites: panels are mounted on racks several feet above the water, carried on piles fixed in the bed. Nothing rests on the water surface. The open water beneath stays in contact with air and wind, which keeps the oxygen penalty small, but also means less evaporation is saved than a floating cover would save.

Sites

Little Dell Reservoir (drinking water)

  • Capacity: 20,500 acre-feet, ≈249 surface acres
  • Current use: Culinary supply, flood control, wildlife habitat
  • Strategy: Demonstration (5% coverage, 5 MW) + scaling (25% coverage, 25 MW)
  • Key benefit: Distributed generation near load; water-quality intervention via algal suppression; best-instrumented over-water PV research site in the Intermountain West
  • Challenge: Culinary source means zero contamination risk is non-negotiable; at an 82 ft mean depth, piles are practical only in the shallower margins, so a bathymetric survey decides feasibility

Great Salt Lake (sovereign lands)

  • Stage: ≈4,191.6 ft (6.4 ft below 4,198 ft healthy target)
  • Strategy: Playa ground-mount primary (1,605 acres, 402 MW_dc, 743,000 MWh/yr) + elevated, pile-mounted research module over the water (5 acres, 2 MW_dc)
  • Key benefit: Material generation (≈83,000 households); dust suppression on exposed lakebed; avian impact research
  • Governing principle: No infrastructure that opposes lake recovery; all siting above 4,200 ft contour (lake recovery reserve)
  • Challenge: A large open-water array is defeated by the lake's 24-ft stage range, brine corrosion, ecology, and public trust

Phased Approach

Phase 0 — Research & Credibility (Months 0–3) - Literature synthesis - University partnership - Screening model validation

Phase 1 — Policy Insertion & Instrumentation (Months 3–12) - Comment on CMP update (time-critical) - Instrument existing projects (Signal Hill) - Publish workshop synthesis

Phase 2 — Funded Demonstration (Months 12–30) - Build small pilot at non-culinary water surface - Agrivoltaics demonstration on working farm - Publish results including negative findings

Phase 3 — Structure the Venture (Months 24–42) - Advisory entity, research institute, or development company - Convert Phase 2 credibility into operational entity

Phase 4 — Scale to Named Sites (Year 3+) - Little Dell: approach with materials/water-quality evidence package - Great Salt Lake: approach only with evidence thresholds met

Key Insight

Agrivoltaics on Utah farmland is a more powerful Great Salt Lake water intervention than solar over the Great Salt Lake itself.

  • Shading irrigated crops reduces crop ET 20–50% in arid climates
  • That is a reduction in consumptive use — water legally dedicable instream
  • Utah has a functioning legal channel: water-leasing and change-application framework

The strongest thesis: not "cover a lake" but "what is the marginal cost of a saved acre-foot and delivered MWh, jointly, across the portfolio?"
