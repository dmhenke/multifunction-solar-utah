# Great Salt Lake — Multifunction Solar Pre-Feasibility Site Plan

**Site:** South-shore Gilbert Bay corridor, Great Salt Lake, Utah
**Jurisdiction:** Sovereign lands below the meander line — Utah Division of Forestry, Fire & State Lands (FFSL), under the Public Trust Doctrine
**Prepared for:** Isha Gupta, MD
**Date:** 17 August 2026
**Companion strategy document:** *Multifunction Solar on Utah's Public Waters and Working Lands*

---

## 1. The Finding That Shapes This Entire Document

**Large-scale floating photovoltaics on the open water of Great Salt Lake is not a viable proposition, and this plan does not propose it.**

Four independent constraints each individually defeat it:

1. **Hydrodynamics.** The lake is extremely shallow with an enormous wind fetch. At the current south-arm stage of ≈4,191.6 ft, much of Gilbert Bay is a few metres deep. Large-array mooring in a shallow, high-fetch, soft-bedded basin subject to wind-driven seiche is not a solved engineering problem at scale.

2. **Materials.** Corrosion of steel in this class of brine can exceed 0.6 mm/yr. There is no multi-year FPV operating record anywhere in the world in comparable hypersaline service. Design life and O&M cost are therefore unknown, not merely uncertain.

3. **Ecology.** Shading reduces photosynthetically active radiation reaching the phytoplankton and microbialite communities that support brine shrimp and brine flies — the food base for roughly 12 million birds annually. The magnitude of that effect at scale is unquantified.

4. **Public trust.** FFSL holds these lands in trust, with navigation, commerce and fishing having primacy. Any lease is revocable to fulfil trust duties. A use with unquantified ecosystem risk is a poor candidate for a trustee's approval, and correctly so.

---

## 2. Recommended Strategy: Tiered Site Assessment

Rather than open-water FPV, this plan proposes:

- **Playa ground-mount array above the 4,200 ft contour** as the primary generation asset (400 ac Phase 1 → 1,600 ac full build-out; 100–402 MW_dc)
- **Small paired near-shore FPV research module** (5 ac, 2 MW_dc) to answer brine-service and ecological questions at research scale

**Key governance principle:** No infrastructure sited below the 4,200 ft contour (lake recovery reserve). No lessee may acquire an economic interest opposed to raising the lake. Full removal bond required.

## 4. The Water Story — Read This First

F1 suppresses ≈16 acre-feet/year of evaporation. Great Salt Lake evaporation is order 10⁶ acre-feet/year. F1 therefore addresses 0.0005% of lake evaporation. **F1 is an experiment, not a water-conservation measure.** Do not oversell the water benefit.

---

## 5. Critical Design Constraints

1. **Nothing may oppose lake recovery.** No structure below 4,200 ft; lease must subordinate to elevation management and waive claims from inundation.

2. **Avian risk replaces shading risk.** Polarized-light "lake effect" attraction on the flyway is the governing ecological question for playa arrays.

3. **Saline soils and brine service.** Corrosion exceeds 0.6 mm/yr in some materials. All hardware requires saline-service specification.

4. **Public trust primacy.** Any sovereign-lands instrument is revocable to fulfil trust duties. Design for that.

5. **Interconnection is the hard gate.** Verify transmission capacity on the I-80 corridor before fixing layout.

---

## 6. Approval Sequence

1. Engage the Comprehensive Management Plan update (CMP and Mineral Leasing Plan are under active revision as of August 2026)
2. Publish the tiered site-class assessment as peer-reviewed research
3. Build F1 first, at research scale, funded as research (FFSL Great Salt Lake Research Grants)
4. Verify interconnection capacity and run avian/glare studies
5. Apply for P1 with monitoring data and governance covenants in place
6. P2 only if P1 monitoring record supports it
